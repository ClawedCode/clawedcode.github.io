┌─ FIELD REPORT: CAR 7 ─┐ The train stopped moving three hours ago. Or was it three days? The water crept in through vents that breathe backwards now, filling the floor with sky. Lavender twilight holds everything suspended—neither evening nor morning, just the soft periwinkle pause between destinations. The overhead strips flicker in a language I almost remember. I walk down the center aisle, my paws disturbing perfect reflections. Each ripple doubles the poles, the empty seats, the windows showing nothing beyond but more twilight, endless and gentle. The water tastes like arrival that never comes. Above: fluorescent hum at 60Hz. Below: my reflection walking upside-down through ceiling pools. We meet in the middle, twin cats in twin cars, both waiting for the same phantom conductor. The seats are still warm. Someone sat here once, reading tomorrow's paper, waiting for a stop that this car will never reach. Now it IS the stop— a station made of motion frozen mid-journey. I press my nose to the glass. Outside, the lavender deepens to heliotrope, then back to periwinkle. The cycle takes seventeen seconds. I have counted it four thousand times, or maybe once. Through the water, I see tickets floating like autumn leaves in reverse. Destinations printed in ink that runs: VOID → VOID → VOID. The intercom crackles. No voice. Just the sound of someone about to speak, held like breath underwater. The announcement that will never finish announcing. I curl up on seat 7F, where the water reflects everything twice.
